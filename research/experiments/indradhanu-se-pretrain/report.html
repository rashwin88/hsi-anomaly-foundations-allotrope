Indradhanu SpectralEarth pretraining: five runs, one stem to carry forward

Every run trained on the same 30,000 SpectralEarth patches with the same SegFormer, schedule and batch, and changed only the spectral stem: the layers that squeeze each pixel's spectrum into 32 numbers and expand it back. They were scored on reconstruction of held-out SpectralEarth sites and on anomaly detection in the four labelled PRISMA scenes.

Why E004 failed on its own bands, and what fixed it

E004 scored set1 below chance on PRISMA's own bands (0.239) and well above production when the same scene was resampled (0.797). Set1 is mostly sea, and E004 painted a land-like near-infrared over it. A water census ruled out the obvious cause: SpectralEarth is 6–11% water, about the same as EnMAP. The cause was the band list. The stem's wavelength networks had only ever seen SpectralEarth's 202 bands, and its skip path gave each band one vote, so a sensor with fewer visible bands got a different mix. Thinning SpectralEarth's own bands to PRISMA's spacing doubled E004's water error with no new sensor involved.

E005 added three fixes: each band counts by the nanometres it covers rather than one vote each; 80% of training batches read a random subset of bands and must still reconstruct all 202; and stated wavelengths are jittered by up to ±3 nm. The loss is only taken on pixels hidden by the mask, so a pixel never learns to fill in its own missing bands.

The runs

All on the kept g6e.2xlarge in ap-south-1b at $2.69 per hour, batch 8 × 4 accumulation, bf16, se-v1 train shards 000000–000199 and test 000000–000009. Throughput is set by data loading and the shared SegFormer, so the stems differ in speed by under 10%.

What we learned

  1. The stem is where the gain is. The linear stem pretrained on SpectralEarth (E001) detects exactly as well as production (0.663 vs 0.662). The adaptive stems add 0.08–0.09.
  2. Reconstruction and detection disagree. The gate stem (E002) reconstructs SpectralEarth best at every epoch, but detects 0.015 below the reduced-attention stem. The pre-registered rule prefers B if it is within seed noise of C, and that noise was never measured. B also cannot read any band list except SpectralEarth's.
  3. A wavelength-aware stem must be trained on many band lists. Without that, E004 fit SpectralEarth's list and failed on PRISMA's. With it, E005 reads PRISMA's own bands at 0.752, level with E004's best resampled score.
  4. Set1 is a volatile scene. Its resampled score swung from 0.44 to 0.80 across E004's epochs and dropped from 0.721 to 0.523 between E005's epochs 10 and 20. One water-dominated scene with 153 tiny objects is weak evidence alone.

Open before the final pretraining run

  • Training data size (these runs used 30,000 of 512,849 patches) and where to run it (the kept L40S, about 2.6 hours per full epoch, or the H200).
  • Why E005's resampled set1 score fell between epochs 10 and 20.

Open before fine-tuning

  • A trained-wavelength record in the checkpoint, extended by each training stage, so EnMAP and PRISMA fine-tuning can bring back the bands SpectralEarth lacks, including the ~1730 nm hydrocarbon band.
  • The EnMAP shards blank a few bands in 85–95% of pixels via the provider's pixel mask; SpectralEarth has none.
  • AVIRIS-NG samples twice as densely as SpectralEarth; check own bands vs resampled on a real scene first.
