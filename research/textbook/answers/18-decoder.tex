\begin{solution}{ex:decoder:1}
Scale $s = 2/4 = 0.5$, so the positions are the same as in \cref{we:decoder:lerp}:
\begin{align*}
d = 0:&\quad u = -0.25 \to 0, & \text{value} &= 2,\\
d = 1:&\quad u = 0.25, & \text{value} &= 0.75 \cdot 2 + 0.25 \cdot 10 = 1.5 + 2.5 = 4,\\
d = 2:&\quad u = 0.75, & \text{value} &= 0.25 \cdot 2 + 0.75 \cdot 10 = 0.5 + 7.5 = 8,\\
d = 3:&\quad u = 1.25, & \text{value} &= 10 \ (\text{last value}).
\end{align*}
Row: $[2, 4, 8, 10]$.

For the grid, first each row: $[0, 4] \to [0, 1, 3, 4]$ (as in the chapter) and $[8, 12] \to [8, 9, 11,
12]$ (since $0.75 \cdot 8 + 0.25 \cdot 12 = 6 + 3 = 9$ and $0.25 \cdot 8 + 0.75 \cdot 12 = 2 + 9 = 11$).
Then down each column with weights $0, 0.25, 0.75, 1$ on the lower row:
\begin{align*}
\text{out row 1} &= 0.75 \cdot [0,1,3,4] + 0.25 \cdot [8,9,11,12]\\
&= [0+2,\ 0.75+2.25,\ 2.25+2.75,\ 3+3] = [2, 3, 5, 6],\\
\text{out row 2} &= 0.25 \cdot [0,1,3,4] + 0.75 \cdot [8,9,11,12]\\
&= [0+6,\ 0.25+6.75,\ 0.75+8.25,\ 1+9] = [6, 7, 9, 10].
\end{align*}
\[
\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & 3 & 4\\ 2 & 3 & 5 & 6\\ 6 & 7 & 9 & 10\\ 8 & 9 & 11 & 12 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{ex:decoder:2}

offset 0 at $(0,0)$, 1 at $(0,1)$, 2 at $(1,0)$, 3 at $(1,1)$. So the output is

\begin{solution}{ex:decoder:3}
Output $(31, 127, 0)$: $h = \lfloor 127/4 \rfloor = 31$, $i = 127 \bmod 4 = 3$, $w = \lfloor 0/4 \rfloor = 0$,
$j = 0 \bmod 4 = 0$. Channel $= 31 \cdot 16 + 4 \cdot 3 + 0 = 496 + 12 = 508$, at position $(31, 0)$.

Input channel 37 at $(2, 3)$: output channel $c = \lfloor 37/16 \rfloor = 2$, offset $37 - 32 = 5$, so
$i = \lfloor 5/4 \rfloor = 1$ and $j = 5 \bmod 4 = 1$. Destination: $(c, Y, X) = (2,\ 4 \cdot 2 + 1,\ 4
\cdot 3 + 1) = (2, 9, 13)$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{ex:decoder:4}
The last convolution outputs $D \times 16 = 24 \times 16 = 384$ channels. Before pixel shuffle one patch
holds $384 \times 32 \times 32 = 384 \times 1024 = 393{,}216$ numbers. The decoder's output is
$24 \times 128 \times 128 = 24 \times 16{,}384 = 393{,}216$ numbers: the same, as a permutation requires.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{ex:decoder:5}
With 256 $\times$ 256 the stage grids are $64, 32, 16, 8$ (\cref{ex:encoder:1}).
\begin{enumerate}
\item mask: \shape{(2, 165, 256, 256)};
\item normalise: \shape{(2, 165, 256, 256)};
\item compress: \shape{(2, 32, 256, 256)};
\item encode: stage 1 has $64 \times 64 = 4096$ tokens, of which $\lfloor 0.65 \times 4096 \rfloor =
\lfloor 2662.4 \rfloor = 2662$ are hidden and $1434$ kept; F1 \shape{(2, 32, 64, 64)}, F2 \shape{(2, 64,
32, 32)}, F3 \shape{(2, 160, 16, 16)}, F4 \shape{(2, 256, 8, 8)};
\item decode: everything at F1's 64 $\times$ 64, concatenation \shape{(2, 1024, 64, 64)}, last
convolution \shape{(2, 512, 64, 64)}, pixel shuffle \shape{(2, 32, 256, 256)};
\item decompress: \shape{(2, 165, 256, 256)};
\item denormalise: \shape{(2, 165, 256, 256)}.
\end{enumerate}
The parameter count is the same as for 128-pixel patches; only the shapes and the work change.
\end{solution}
