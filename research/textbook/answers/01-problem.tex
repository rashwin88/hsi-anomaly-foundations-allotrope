\begin{solution}{ex:problem:1}
(1) No labelled anomalies exist, because labelling a satellite pixel needs someone to have
confirmed what is on the ground; this closes the route ``collect training data''. (2) The target
class is open-ended: a classifier can only return labels it was given, so it is blind to the
unanticipated anomaly that matters most; this closes the route ``label a few known kinds and
hope''. (3) Anomalies are so rare that a rule answering ``normal'' every time scores near-perfect
accuracy; this closes the route ``train anyway and check the accuracy'', because the check itself
is meaningless.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{ex:problem:2}
The rule is right on every normal pixel and wrong on every anomalous one.
\begin{align*}
\text{normal pixels} &= 1{,}000{,}000 - 10 = 999{,}990,\\
\text{accuracy} &= \frac{999{,}990}{1{,}000{,}000} = 0.99999 = 99.999\%,\\
\text{base rate} &= \frac{10}{1{,}000{,}000} = 0.00001 = 10^{-5}.
\end{align*}
The rule finds none of the ten anomalies and still scores $99.999\%$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{ex:problem:3}
Mean:
\begin{align*}
10 + 12 + 14 + 20 &= 56,\\
\mu &= 56/4 = 14.
\end{align*}
Differences from the mean and their squares:
\begin{align*}
10 - 14 &= -4, & 16,\\
12 - 14 &= -2, & 4,\\
14 - 14 &= 0, & 0,\\
20 - 14 &= 6, & 36.
\end{align*}
Average of the squares and its root:
\begin{align*}
16 + 4 + 0 + 36 &= 56,\\
\sigma^2 &= 56/4 = 14,\\
\sigma &= \sqrt{14} \approx 3.742.
\end{align*}
Score of the pixel reading $20$:
\[ s = \frac{\abs{20 - 14}}{3.742} = \frac{6}{3.742} \approx 1.60. \]
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{ex:problem:4}
\begin{align*}
k &= 0.0005 \times 2{,}000{,}000 = 1{,}000 \text{ pixels.}
\end{align*}
Doubling every score does not change the kept set. Multiplying all scores by the same positive
number preserves their order: if $s_a > s_b$ then $2s_a > 2s_b$. The top $1{,}000$ by rank are the
same pixels; only the cut-off value (the $99.95$ percentile) doubles with them.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{ex:problem:5}
Differences band by band:
\begin{align*}
0.05 - 0.07 &= -0.02,\\
0.25 - 0.22 &= 0.03,\\
0.40 - 0.40 &= 0.00.
\end{align*}
Absolute values $0.02$, $0.03$, $0.00$; their sum is $0.05$; divided by $3$ the score is
$0.05/3 \approx 0.0167$.

A pixel stored as $0$ is not a measurement, but its values are far from every real pixel, so any
score that measures distance from normal ranks it near the top. It also distorts the scene
statistics (mean and spread) that the genuine pixels are scored against, as
\cref{we:problem:unmasked} showed, so it must be excluded both from the statistics and from the
ranking.
\end{solution}
