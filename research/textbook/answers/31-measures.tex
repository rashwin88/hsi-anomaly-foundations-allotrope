\begin{solution}{ex:measures:1}
$P = 2$ targets (0.6, 0.3), $N = 2$ background pixels (0.5, 0.4). Sweep the threshold down:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcccc}
\toprule
threshold & TP & FP & TPR & FPR \\
\midrule
above 0.6 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\
0.6 (T) & 1 & 0 & 1/2 & 0 \\
0.5 (B) & 1 & 1 & 1/2 & 1/2 \\
0.4 (B) & 1 & 2 & 1/2 & 1 \\
0.3 (T) & 2 & 2 & 1 & 1 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
ROC points: $(0,0), (0,\frac12), (\frac12,\frac12), (1,\frac12), (1,1)$.

Trapezoids:
\begin{align*}
(0,0)\to(0,\tfrac12):&\ \text{width } 0 \to 0\\
(0,\tfrac12)\to(\tfrac12,\tfrac12):&\ \tfrac12 \times \tfrac{\frac12+\frac12}{2} = \tfrac12 \times \tfrac12 = \tfrac14\\
(\tfrac12,\tfrac12)\to(1,\tfrac12):&\ \tfrac12 \times \tfrac12 = \tfrac14\\
(1,\tfrac12)\to(1,1):&\ \text{width } 0 \to 0\\
\text{AUC} &= \tfrac14 + \tfrac14 = \tfrac12.
\end{align*}
Pairs ($2 \times 2 = 4$): target 0.6 beats 0.5 and 0.4 (2 wins); target 0.3 beats neither (0 wins). AUC $= 2/4 =
0.5$.

$\mathrm{pAUC}_{0.01}$: the curve is at TPR $\frac12$ from FPR 0 to $\frac12$, so the area to 0.01 is $0.01 \times
\frac12 = 0.005$, and $0.005 / 0.01 = 0.5$. $\mathrm{TPR}@0.001$: the points with FPR $\le 0.001$ are $(0,0)$ and
$(0, \frac12)$, so the best TPR is $\frac12$.

AUC (0.5) says ``random''; pAUC (0.5, a hundred times its chance of 0.005) says the top of the list is excellent.
Half the targets are at the very top and half at the very bottom: AUC averages the two, the top-of-list measures
see only the first.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{ex:measures:2}
Chance: the diagonal gives $\frac1m\int_0^m f\,df = \frac1m \cdot \frac{m^2}{2} = \frac m2 = \frac{0.05}{2} = 0.025$.

McClish for $A = 0.02$:
\begin{align*}
A_{\min} &= m^2/2 = 0.05^2 / 2 = 0.0025 / 2 = 0.00125\\
A_{\max} &= m = 0.05\\
\frac{A - A_{\min}}{A_{\max} - A_{\min}} &= \frac{0.02 - 0.00125}{0.05 - 0.00125} = \frac{0.01875}{0.04875} = 0.3846\\
\mathrm{pAUC}^{\text{McClish}} &= \tfrac12(1 + 0.3846) = \tfrac12 \times 1.3846 = 0.6923.
\end{align*}
On the repository's scale the same area is $0.02 / 0.05 = 0.40$ (chance 0.025).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{ex:measures:3}
Cells (0,0), (1,0), (1,1). Their 4-neighbours not in the object:
\begin{itemize}
\item from (0,0): $(-1,0)$, $(0,-1)$, $(0,1)$ [$(1,0)$ is in the object];
\item from (1,0): $(2,0)$, $(1,-1)$ [$(0,0)$ and $(1,1)$ are in the object];
\item from (1,1): $(2,1)$, $(1,2)$ [$(0,1)$ already counted; $(1,0)$ in the object].
\end{itemize}
That is $3 + 2 + 2 = 7$ neighbours, so $m = 3 + 7 = 10$.
\begin{align*}
0.99^{10} &= 0.99^5 \times 0.99^5 = 0.95099 \times 0.95099 = 0.90438\\
P(\text{caught}) &= 1 - 0.90438 = 0.0956.
\end{align*}
About 9.6\%; the straight three-in-a-row (m = 11) gets 10.5\% because a straight line exposes one more neighbour.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{ex:measures:4}
\begin{align*}
n_{\text{pts}} &= \min(256, \max(20, 600{,}000 /\!/ 10{,}000 + 64)) = \min(256, \max(20, 60 + 64)) = 124\\
\text{spacing} &= 100 / (124 - 1) = 100 / 123 = 0.813 \text{ percentile.}
\end{align*}
The sampled thresholds below the maximum flag the top 0.813\%, then 1.626\%, \ldots{} So only one sampled point
lies between 0 and 1\% of the pixels, and the first-1\% area is a single straight-line guess.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{ex:measures:5}
\begin{align*}
\text{chance, 1 pixel} &= 1 - 0.99^5 = 0.049\\
\text{chance, domino} &= 1 - 0.99^8 = 0.077\\
\text{mean} &= (0.049 + 0.077)/2 = 0.126/2 = 0.063\\
12\% / 6.3\% &= 0.12 / 0.063 = 1.9.
\end{align*}
The model catches 1.9 times as many objects as random scores would.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{ex:measures:6}
Background pixels in descending order: 0.5 and 0.4. When the walk reaches 0.5, one target (0.6) has been passed:
TPR $\frac12$. When it reaches 0.4, still one target: TPR $\frac12$. Each rectangle has width $1/N = \frac12$:
\[
\text{AUC} = \tfrac12 \times \tfrac12 + \tfrac12 \times \tfrac12 = \tfrac14 + \tfrac14 = \tfrac12.
\]
The same 0.5 as the trapezoids and the pair count.
\end{solution}
